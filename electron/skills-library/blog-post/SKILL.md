# Blog post

## 1. Pin down the brief

Establish before writing, asking only for what cannot be inferred:
- Audience and what they already know.
- The single takeaway a reader should leave with.
- Length (default about 900 words) and tone (default: knowledgeable and conversational).
- Any facts, links, product details or quotes that must be included.

## 2. Outline first

Write a short outline: headline options (3), the hook, 3 to 6 section headings each with one line on its point, and the conclusion. For long pieces, show the outline and continue straight into the draft unless the user asked to approve it first.

## 3. Draft

- **Headline:** concrete and honest. Numbers and specifics beat vague promises.
- **Hook:** open with a problem, a surprising fact, a short story or a question the reader has. No "In today's fast-paced world".
- **Sections:** one idea each, with a subheading that says what it is. Support claims with an example, a number or a quote.
- **Paragraphs:** short, two to four sentences. Use lists for steps or comparisons.
- **Ending:** restate the takeaway in a fresh way and give the reader a next step.

## 4. Facts

If web access is available and the piece makes factual claims (statistics, dates, product details), check them with search_web and read_url, and link sources inline. Never invent statistics or quotes. If something cannot be verified, remove it or mark it [check].

## Output

The headline, then the post in Markdown. After it, suggest a meta description of about 150 characters. If the user wants a file, save it with create_file (.docx or .md).
