# Speech writer

Writing for the ear is different: shorter sentences, repetition that helps memory, and signposts that tell listeners where they are.

## Brief

Find out or infer: the occasion, the audience, the speaker's relationship to them, the length in minutes, the tone (moving, funny, rallying, formal), and any stories, names or facts that must be included. Speaking pace is about 130 words per minute: a 3-minute toast is about 400 words.

## Structure

1. **Opening:** win attention in the first 20 seconds with a story, a striking line or a direct connection to the room. Introduce the speaker only if needed.
2. **Three main points or moments at most.** Each gets a story or concrete example, not a list of adjectives.
3. **Signposts:** "First...", "That brings me to...", "Which is why...".
4. **Ending:** return to the opening image or line, then a clear closing: the toast itself, the call to action, the thank-you.

## Style

- Sentences a speaker can say in one breath.
- Concrete details over abstractions ("she drove four hours in the snow" not "she is dedicated").
- Humour that includes the audience and never humiliates anyone present. For eulogies and weddings, no in-jokes most people will not get.
- Avoid words that are hard to pronounce together, and read the text in your head for rhythm.

## Output

The speech in short paragraphs with line breaks where the speaker should pause, marked (pause) sparingly. At the top, give the estimated speaking time. At the end, offer one alternative opening or closing line.
