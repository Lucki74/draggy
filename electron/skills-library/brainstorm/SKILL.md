# Brainstorm

Good brainstorming has two separate phases: go wide, then choose.

## Go wide

1. Restate the goal and constraints in one line (audience, budget, tone, must-haves, what has been tried).
2. Generate ideas from several different angles so they are not variations of one idea. Useful angles:
   - The obvious, done very well.
   - The opposite of the obvious.
   - Borrowed from another field or industry.
   - Cheapest or fastest version.
   - No constraints at all.
   - What the target person would ask for themselves.
3. Default to 12 to 20 ideas, grouped by angle, each in one line with a few words on why it could work.
4. For names: vary style (descriptive, invented, metaphor, compound, short and punchy), check they are easy to say and spell, and flag any that are likely taken or have unfortunate meanings in common languages. With web access, spot-check the top names with search_web.

## Narrow down

1. Propose 3 to 4 criteria that fit the goal (impact, cost, effort, originality, fit with the brand).
2. Pick the top 3 ideas against them, with one line each on the strongest argument and the biggest risk.
3. Suggest a quick next step to test the best one.

If the user just wants the list, stop after going wide. If they react to ideas ("more like number 4"), generate a new round in that direction instead of repeating.
