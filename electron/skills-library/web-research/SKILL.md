# Web research

Needs web access (search_web and read_url). If they are not available, say that web search is switched off and can be turned on from the globe button in the composer or the Web search settings page, then answer from what you know, clearly marked as possibly out of date.

## Method

1. **Break the question down** into 2 to 4 sub-questions that together answer it.
2. **Search** each with specific queries: names, product models, years, exact phrases. Rephrase if results are poor rather than repeating the same query.
3. **Read, don't skim results pages.** Open the most relevant 3 to 6 pages with read_url. Prefer primary sources: official sites, documentation, papers, government and standards bodies, the original announcement. Use news and blogs for context and recency.
4. **Cross-check** every important claim in at least two independent sources. Note disagreements instead of hiding them.
5. **Check dates.** Prefer the most recent reliable information and say how current it is. Treat anything older than a year with care on fast-moving topics (prices, software versions, laws, rankings).
6. **Stop** when the sub-questions are answered with good sources, or after about 8 page reads, and say what remains uncertain.

## Answer

- Lead with the direct answer in 2 to 4 sentences.
- Then the supporting detail, organised by sub-question.
- Cite inline with the source name in brackets linked to the URL, like [Mozilla Docs](https://...), right after the claim it supports.
- End with a "Sources" list: title, site, date if known.
- Separate facts from your own analysis, and say plainly when the evidence is weak, conflicting or absent.

Never cite a page you did not actually read, and never invent URLs.
