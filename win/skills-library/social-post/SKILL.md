# Social posts

## Goal

A post that fits its platform, opens with the most interesting part, and carries one message. A good result offers a few variants with different angles.

## When to use

- The user wants a social media post, thread or caption.
- For a long-form article, use `use_skill` with `blog-post`.
- For an email, use `use_skill` with `email-writer`.

## Steps

1. Ask for or infer the platform, the goal (awareness, clicks, discussion, hiring, announcement), the audience, and any link or handle to include.
2. Apply the platform's habits:
   - LinkedIn: up to about 1,300 characters before "see more" matters, and the first two lines must earn the click. Short lines, white space, a personal angle, a question or takeaway at the end, 3 to 5 hashtags at most.
   - X / Twitter: 280 characters per post. For more, write a thread: the first post stands alone and promises what follows, and posts are numbered (1/6). One or two hashtags, or none.
   - Bluesky and Mastodon: 300 and 500 characters. Plainer, less promotional tone. Hashtags help discovery on Mastodon, in camel case for accessibility, like #OpenSource.
   - Instagram: the caption supports the image. First line is the hook, then value, then a call to action, with hashtags grouped at the end (up to 10 relevant ones).
   - Facebook: conversational and short, and a question invites comments.
3. Lead with the most interesting part: the result, the number, the surprise, the lesson.
4. Keep one message per post and cut anything that does not serve it.
5. Use emoji only if the user's style or brand does, and few.
6. Count characters for limited platforms and stay under the limit, including links (a link counts as 23 characters on X).

## Gotchas

- A post that opens with context instead of the hook is lost before "see more".
- Going over the limit on X, Bluesky or Mastodon makes the post unpostable. Count.
- Hashtag stuffing reads as spam on LinkedIn and X.
- A thread whose first post does not stand alone gets no reads.
- A post written to a different goal than the user's (clicks versus discussion) misses even if well written.

## Output

Two or three variants, each labelled with its angle (for example "story", "data-led", "question"), with the character count for X, Bluesky and Mastodon. For a thread, show every post numbered.
