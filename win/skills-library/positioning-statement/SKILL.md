# Positioning statement

## Goal

A short internal statement that fixes who the product is for, what it is, why it is different and why anyone should believe it. Copy, sales talk and product choices then agree with it.

## When to use

- The user cannot explain in one breath what the business does or why it is better.
- Messaging across site, sales and ads is inconsistent.
- For the customer-facing promise built from jobs and pains, use `use_skill` with `value-proposition`.
- For a plan to launch with the chosen position, use `use_skill` with `go-to-market-plan`.
- The user is rebranding or pivoting and must restate what the company is.

## Steps

1. Ask with `ask_user` for the product, its best customers, three rivals or alternatives (including doing nothing), and what customers say when they praise it. Read any notes with `read_file`.
2. Choose the target. One narrow group with a shared need. If the user lists several, ask which brings the most value and start there.
3. Choose the frame of reference: the category buyers use to place you. Ask what they would search for or what they compare you to. A category that is too new needs more explaining; one that is too crowded hides you.
4. Find the unique value: the one benefit that matters most to the target and that rivals do not offer in the same way. Test with "so what?" until the answer is a customer outcome.
5. Gather proof: facts that support the claim, such as customer results the user can show, certifications, a unique method, or years of work. Use only what the user can document. Do not invent figures.
6. Write the statement in this form: "For [target] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [alternative], it [difference], because [proof]."
7. Test it against rivals: could a rival sign the same sentence? If yes, sharpen the difference. Test it against the target: would they say "that is me"?
8. Check each word. Replace jargon, superlatives and empty terms (leading, innovative, best-in-class) with specifics.
9. Draft three one-line versions for outward use (website header, sales opener, short bio) that follow the statement without repeating it word for word.
10. Ask the user to read it aloud and say whether they could defend each clause to a skeptical customer.

## Gotchas

- Positioning on features invites a feature race. Position on the outcome and who it is for.
- Claiming to be best at several things positions you at nothing. Choose one.
- A statement that no rival could also say is rare. If every clause fits several rivals, it is not positioning yet.
- Proof that is only adjectives is not proof. Ask for an example, a number the user owns or a named customer who agreed to be cited.
- The statement is internal. The public line comes from it, but is not it.
- Revisit when the target or rivals change.
- Ask three customers to describe the product in their own words. Gaps between that and the statement show what to fix.

## Output

The one-paragraph statement, the five parts listed under it (Target, Category, Benefit, Alternative, Proof), three outward-facing lines, and a short list of weak spots left to resolve.
