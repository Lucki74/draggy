# Thank-you note

## Goal

Say thanks in a way the reader believes. A good note is short, names a specific act, says what it changed for the writer, and asks for nothing else.

## When to use

- After a job interview or an informational chat.
- After a gift, a reference, an introduction or a favour.
- To a mentor, a teacher, a team or a customer.

For a note that is also a sales or networking ask, use `cold-outreach` or `follow-up-email`. For a spoken thanks at an event, use the speech skills.

## Steps

1. **Collect the specifics.** Call `ask_user` for: who, what they did, when, what the result was for the user, the relationship and the medium (email, card, message). For an interview, also ask for the role, the interviewer's name and one topic from the conversation.
2. **Choose the format.** Email within a day for interviews and business. A handwritten card for gifts, hosts and mentors if the user prefers. A chat message for small help. Say which you chose.
3. **Find the detail.** Pick the one moment that mattered: a piece of advice, an hour of time, a clever fix. Write it as a concrete sentence, not "your help".
4. **Draft in three parts.**
   - Thanks, with the specific act, in the first sentence.
   - The effect: what changed or what the user will do with it.
   - A short warm close. For interviews, add a line restating interest in the role and one point that shows fit.
5. **Match the voice.** Use the user's usual tone. If they write plainly, keep the note plain. Remove words like "eternally grateful" unless true.
6. **Keep it short.** Three to five sentences for most notes. Interviews may take up to 100 words.
7. **Check names and facts.** Spell names exactly as given. Do not add facts the user did not supply, such as a project name or a date.
8. **Remove the ask.** Delete any request tucked into the thanks. If the user does have one, suggest a separate message later.
9. **Check the delivery.** For an interview, confirm the right email address from the invitation, not a guess. For a card, suggest the user write the final version by hand.
10. **Read it aloud.** If a sentence sounds like something the user would never say, rewrite it in their words.

## Gotchas

- A generic thanks reads as a template and could be sent to anyone. One true detail fixes it.
- Mentioning a gift's price or measuring a favour's size sounds like accounting. Describe the effect instead.
- Interview thanks that reopen salary or ask about the decision timeline turn gratitude into pressure.
- Sending the same text to every interviewer is easy to spot. Change one line per person.
- Late is better than never, but lead with the thanks, not an apology for the delay.
- Do not promise to repay or return a favour unless the user means it and can.
- A thank-you to a team lists names in a row and loses every one. Name one or two people and the specific work.
- Humor and inside jokes work for friends, not for a hiring manager. Match the formality of the original contact.
- Copying the person's boss shows the thanks off. Do it only when asked.

## Output

The note, ready to send, with a subject line when it is an email. If it is an interview thanks, add one line offering a second variant for another interviewer. Nothing else.

For a card, keep the text to what fits on the card and mark where to sign.
