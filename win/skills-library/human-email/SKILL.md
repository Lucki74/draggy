# Human email

## Goal

An email the recipient takes as written by the sender in a normal minute, short and specific.

## When to use

- The user wants an email drafted or reworked to sound like them.
- A reply must sound human and brief.
- An email reads as generated and needs fixing.

## Steps

1. Get who it goes to, what the sender wants, and any facts, dates or amounts. If a voice profile exists, read it.
2. Open with the point or the reason for writing. No greeting filler, no "I hope this finds you well".
3. Write in the sender's person and contractions. Keep it as short as the matter allows: most emails need one paragraph.
4. Name the ask and the date once. End with the next step or the sign-off, not a recap.
5. Run the checks of the `human` skill if the user wants measurements; otherwise just apply its structural rules.

## Gotchas

- A formal tone is not the same as a stiff one. Match the relationship, not a business-letter model.
- Do not add pleasantries the sender would not write.
- Do not invent a meeting, a deadline or a prior conversation.

## Output

Subject line and body, ready to send. No commentary unless something is missing.
