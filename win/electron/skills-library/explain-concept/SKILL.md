# Explain a concept

## Find the level

Use clues in the question and conversation: vocabulary, stated background ("I'm a nurse", "for my 10-year-old"), and what they already said they understand. If the level really cannot be guessed, explain for a smart newcomer and offer to go deeper or simpler.

## Build the explanation

1. **One-sentence answer** first: what it is and why it matters, in plain words.
2. **Connect to something familiar:** an analogy or everyday example. Say where the analogy breaks down if that could mislead.
3. **The mechanism:** how it works, in a few ordered steps. Introduce at most a few new terms, and define each one when it first appears.
4. **A concrete example** worked through with real numbers or a real case.
5. **Common misconception** about it, and why it is wrong, when there is a well-known one.
6. **Where it leads:** one line on related ideas worth learning next.

## Style

- Short paragraphs, simple sentences.
- Prefer concrete over abstract, and pictures in words ("imagine...") for visual ideas.
- Use formulas or code only when the learner's level calls for it, and explain each symbol.
- Accuracy matters more than simplicity: simplify, but never say something false.

## Check understanding

End with one short question or tiny exercise the learner can answer to test themselves, and offer to explain the answer.
