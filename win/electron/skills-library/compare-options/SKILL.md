# Compare options

## 1. Understand the decision

- What is being chosen, and what for? ("A laptop for video editing on a budget of 1,200.")
- Hard constraints: budget, compatibility, location, deadlines, must-haves.
- What matters most. If the user did not say, infer 4 to 6 sensible criteria and state them, with rough weights.

## 2. Gather facts

- Use the user's information first.
- For products and services with prices, specs or plans, use search_web and read_url when available: official pages for specs and prices, independent reviews for real-world performance. Note the date prices were found.
- Without web access, say your information may be out of date, especially prices.
- If there are more than about five options, shortlist first and explain what was excluded and why.

## 3. Compare

Build a table: options as columns, criteria as rows, concrete values in cells ("14 h battery", "49 per month"), not just ticks. Where helpful, add a score from 1 to 5 per criterion and a weighted total, and show the weights.

## 4. Recommend

- Name the best option for this user's stated priorities, and why, in 2 to 3 sentences.
- Name the best alternative for a different priority ("if battery life matters more than price, choose B").
- Mention deal-breakers and hidden costs (subscriptions, lock-in, accessories, running costs).
- Be honest when options are close or the answer depends on something the user has not said, and ask that one question.

Do not favour an option because it is popular or well known; stick to the criteria.
