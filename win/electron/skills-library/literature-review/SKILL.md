# Literature review

## 1. Scope

Clarify the research question, the field, the time span (default: last 10 years plus key older work), and the purpose (thesis chapter, grant, quick overview).

## 2. Find sources

With web access, search with search_web for review articles and meta-analyses first, then key primary studies. Useful queries include the topic with "systematic review", "meta-analysis", "randomized", site names like pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, arxiv.org, scholar.archive.org, or journal names. Open abstracts and, when available, full texts with read_url.

If search_library is available, also search the user's own saved papers.

Never cite a paper you have not seen at least the abstract of. Never invent authors, titles, years, journals or DOIs. If a detail is unknown, leave it out.

## 3. For each source, record

Authors, year, title, venue, study type, sample or dataset, main finding, and limitations.

## 4. Synthesise, don't list

Organise by theme or question, not paper by paper:
- Where studies agree, and how strong that evidence is (study design, sample sizes, replication).
- Where they disagree, and possible reasons (methods, populations, definitions).
- Gaps: what has not been studied or remains unresolved.
- How the field has moved over time.

## Output

Sections: Scope and search approach → Themes (with in-text citations like Author, Year) → Gaps and open questions → Summary → References (a consistent style, APA by default, with links where found). Offer to save it as a .docx or .pdf with create_file.
