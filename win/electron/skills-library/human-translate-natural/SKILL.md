# Human translate natural

## Goal

A target-language text a native reader would not suspect is a translation.

## When to use

- The user wants a translation that sounds native.
- An existing translation is stiff or literal.
- Text is being localized for a specific country or audience.

## Steps

1. Ask for the target language and country, the audience and the register. A sample of the user's writing in that language helps most.
2. Translate for meaning and sentence rhythm, not word order. Split or join sentences when the target language does.
3. Keep names, numbers, product terms and quotations unchanged unless the user wants them localized.
4. Read the result as a native reader: idioms that exist in the language, punctuation and date formats of that country.
5. List any term you were unsure about, with the choice you made.

## Gotchas

- The rhythm of the source text carries over by habit. Re-check sentence lengths in the target language.
- Formal and informal address (tu and vous, du and Sie, tu and usted) must match the context. Ask if unclear.
- Do not translate code, URLs or placeholders.

## Output

The translation, then a short list of uncertain terms.
