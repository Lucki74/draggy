# Human fiction voice

## Goal

A scene where the narrator, the details and the dialogue belong to these characters in this place.

## When to use

- The user wants a scene or chapter written or revised.
- A draft has stock emotion lines, tidy endings or dialogue that explains.
- The narrator's voice feels generic.

## Steps

1. Ask for the viewpoint character, the setting, what happens, and a sample paragraph of the user's fiction if they have one.
2. Render feeling as an act, an object or a cost, not as a body sensation. Someone sets the table for one person fewer.
3. Give each speaker a different rhythm. Let dialogue skip what people already know and let characters talk past each other.
4. Use named, specific things: brands, streets, tools, food. Draw them from the user's world.
5. End the scene before it resolves, or on an image. No stated moral.
6. Cut any sentence that says what the scene means.

## Gotchas

- Names, places and facts of the user's world are theirs. Do not invent canon for a series.
- A stock list of three sensory details per scene is a pattern. Use one that matters.
- Do not copy another author's voice by name.

## Output

The scene. Questions about canon go after it.
